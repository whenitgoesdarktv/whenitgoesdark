What to Do When the Power Goes Out: The First Hour

The lights die and the house goes quiet. Most of what keeps people safe in a blackout is boring and happens in the first hour. Here it is, in order, with the source for every number.

Updated October 29, 2026 · Sources: Ready.gov, CDC, FoodSafety.gov

The first 5 moves

  1. Keep the fridge and freezer closed. Every time you open them, you lose cold you can't get back.
  2. Unplug electronics and appliances. When the power comes back, a surge can damage anything left plugged in. Leave one lamp on so you know when it's back.
  3. Use flashlights, not candles. A headlamp keeps both hands free.
  4. Check on family and neighbors, if it's safe to go out. Older neighbors and anyone on a medical device first.
  5. Save your phone. Low power mode, brightness down, and get news from a battery or hand-crank radio instead.
The quiet killer is carbon monoxide. Run generators, grills and camp stoves outside only, at least 20 feet from doors, windows and vents. Never inside your home, garage or carport, even with the door open. Never heat your home with a gas stove or oven. If anyone gets a headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea or confusion, get everyone to fresh air and call 911.

How long is food safe in the fridge without power?

About 4 hours, if the door stays closed. After that, throw out refrigerated meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk and leftovers. Never taste food to check it. When in doubt, throw it out.

How long does a freezer stay cold?

FreezerHolds its temperature for about
Full, door closed48 hours
Half full, door closed24 hours

A full freezer stays cold longer, so filling empty space with containers of water before a storm helps.

How much water do you need?

1 gallon per person, per day, for at least 3 days, for drinking and basic sanitation. Add extra for pets. If you live in a high-rise, water to the upper floors may depend on electric pumps, so no power can mean no water. Fill containers and the bathtub before a storm arrives.

What about medicine that needs a fridge?

If the power is out for a day or more, replace refrigerated medicines unless the label says otherwise. Ask your doctor or pharmacist about a plan before the next outage, not during it.

Too hot or too cold?

  • Heat: go somewhere air-conditioned if you can. Wet your skin, wear light clothes, skip heavy activity.
  • Cold: layers and blankets, and plan where you'll spend the coldest hours. No ovens, stoves or grills for heat.
Stay or leave? If your home gets too hot, too cold or unsafe, going somewhere with power (family, a friend, or a shelter or cooling center your local officials open) is the smart move, not giving up. That exact choice is the vote at the end of every episode of our show.

What to have before it goes dark

  • Water (see above) and 3 days of food that needs no fridge and no cooking, plus a manual can opener
  • A flashlight or headlamp for each person, and extra batteries
  • A battery or hand-crank radio, ideally with NOAA weather alerts
  • A charged power bank
  • A carbon monoxide alarm that runs on batteries or has a battery backup
  • First aid kit, medicines, pet supplies, some cash, and copies of important papers
