By ten, your room is fifty-eight degrees. By midnight, fifty-one.

Loose, warm layers. Towels under the door. Curtains shut. Close off every room you're not using.

Lando ignores the rule. Lando sleeps on your face.

Twelve forty. A knock. It's the woman from room twelve. Rosa. Two scarves, and a hotel bathrobe over her coat.

"Everybody's going down to the lobby," she says. "It's warm down there."

It is warm down there. Six guests asleep on the couches. The generator humming beside the fire. The front door open to the snow.

On the stairs, a headache starts behind your eyes. You figure it's the altitude. Or the day.

Rosa takes the couch closest to the generator.

You carry Lando back upstairs, because Lando will not stop yowling in that lobby. Every time you set him down, he pulls toward the door.

Cats hate loud machines. That's what you tell yourself.

Two forty-seven a.m. You wake up, and the room is turning.

The carrier door is open. Lando is gone.

Then you hear it. Scratching. Inside the wall, right behind the bed.

You press your ear to the wallpaper. Claws. Moving. Fast. Toward the hallway.

You follow it with your headlamp. Twenty doors. Every one of them dark.

The scratching runs ahead of you, past the ice machine, all the way to the end of the hall. And it stops under a metal grate in the floor.

A vent. Warm air is rising out of it. From the lobby.

You kneel down. Two green eyes look back up at you.

Lando.

He isn't trying to get to you. He's trying to get away from that warm air.

You stand up too fast. The hallway tilts. Your stomach turns over. Your fingers can't work the grate.

For a second, someone is standing at the far end of the hall. Your headlamp flickers. There's no one there.

The scratching was Lando.

The dizziness is not.
