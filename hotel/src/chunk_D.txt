You shut the generator off. The silence is enormous.

Rosa leans on you like you're the railing. The others wake up dizzy and confused, and you herd them out under the porch roof. The snow is blowing sideways.

Dale is too heavy to carry. You grab him under the arms and drag him across the lobby floor. Inch by inch. Through the open door. Into the cold.

Lando walks ahead of you like he's leading a parade.

Your phone says nine percent. One bar. You call nine-one-one. It rings. And rings. Then a voice. The road is closed. They'll get to you as fast as they can.

And now there's a new problem. Nine degrees. Seven people in pajamas. One cat.

Shivering. Exhaustion. Confusion. Fumbling hands. Slurred speech. Drowsiness.

Half of those look exactly like carbon monoxide. You can't tell which one is hurting Rosa.

Your car is a white hump in the lot. You dig with your hands until you can't feel them. The door opens. The engine catches. Heat pours out. Four people pile in.

And Lando starts yowling again. The same yowl. He claws at the back window.

You look behind the car. The snow has drifted right over the tailpipe.

Stuck with your car in snow? Clear the snow off the exhaust pipe. Run the engine about ten minutes an hour, and crack a window.
