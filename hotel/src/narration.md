=== CHUNK A ===
Two forty-seven in the morning. The power has been out for six hours.

Your cat's carrier is open. And empty.

Somewhere inside the wall, something is scratching.

It moves. Down the hallway. Past the ice machine. Past room twenty-four.

Then it stops. Right behind your head.

The man at the front desk told you nobody else is staying on this floor.

And downstairs, something has been running all night. You can feel it through the floor. A low, steady hum.

So what's inside the walls? And what is that hum?

Before the sun comes up, one of them is going to try to kill everyone in this hotel.

Nine hours earlier. Six p.m.

The Pinecrest Lodge sits halfway up a mountain road. The sign on the front door says, no pets.

The bag on your shoulder says nothing. Because Lando is asleep inside it.

Lando is your cat. Lando has opinions about bags.

The storm got here first. The radio says a nor'easter is wrapping around the mountains, the plows have stopped for the night, and the road behind you is already gone.

The manager, Dale, slides a key across the desk. Room twenty-two. The last one.

He looks at your bag. The bag meows.

Dale says nothing. Dale has had a very long day.

Seven p.m. The snow is coming down an inch every half hour. Your car is a white hump in the lot. The only way off this mountain is a road you can't see anymore.

Eight twelve p.m. The lights flicker. Once. Twice.

Then the whole mountain goes dark.

No heat. No elevator. No Wi-Fi. Your phone says sixty-four percent.

Then, from downstairs, a motor coughs to life.

You look over the railing. Dale is dragging a generator in from the snow. Into the lobby. Right next to the fireplace.

He props the front door open with a boot.

"It's just for the coffee and the phones," he says. "Door's open. It's fine."

Remember that open door.

A guest in a red hat is lighting candles all along the front desk. Ten of them. Fifteen.

When the power is out, use flashlights, not candles. And in a storm like this, a fire truck is not getting up that road.

You hand her your spare flashlight. She blows the candles out. Lando watches the smoke like it owes him money.

=== CHUNK B ===
By ten, your room is fifty-eight degrees. By midnight, fifty-one.

Loose, warm layers. Towels under the door. Curtains shut. Close off every room you're not using.

Lando ignores the rule. Lando sleeps on your face.

Twelve forty. A knock. It's the woman from room twelve. Rosa. Two scarves, and a hotel bathrobe over her coat.

"Everybody's going down to the lobby," she says. "It's warm down there."

It is warm down there. Six guests asleep on the couches. The generator humming beside the fire. The front door open to the snow.

On the stairs, a headache starts behind your eyes. You figure it's the altitude. Or the day.

Rosa takes the couch closest to the generator.

You carry Lando back upstairs, because Lando will not stop yowling in that lobby. Every time you set him down, he pulls toward the door.

Cats hate loud machines. That's what you tell yourself.

Two forty-seven a.m. You wake up, and the room is turning.

The carrier door is open. Lando is gone.

Then you hear it. Scratching. Inside the wall, right behind the bed.

You press your ear to the wallpaper. Claws. Moving. Fast. Toward the hallway.

You follow it with your headlamp. Twenty doors. Every one of them dark.

The scratching runs ahead of you, past the ice machine, all the way to the end of the hall. And it stops under a metal grate in the floor.

A vent. Warm air is rising out of it. From the lobby.

You kneel down. Two green eyes look back up at you.

Lando.

He isn't trying to get to you. He's trying to get away from that warm air.

You stand up too fast. The hallway tilts. Your stomach turns over. Your fingers can't work the grate.

For a second, someone is standing at the far end of the hall. Your headlamp flickers. There's no one there.

The scratching was Lando.

The dizziness is not.

=== CHUNK C ===
Carbon monoxide has no color and no smell. Headache. Dizziness. Weakness. Nausea. Sleepiness. Confusion. It feels like the flu. And people who are asleep can die from it before they ever feel sick.

Six people are asleep in that lobby.

Every year in the United States, more than a hundred people die from carbon monoxide from portable generators.

You go down the stairs with Lando in one arm and the railing in the other. Every step is heavier than the last.

The lobby is warm. Too warm. The fire is down to coals. The generator is still humming.

"Rosa." She doesn't wake. You shake her shoulder. She opens her eyes, and she doesn't know who you are.

Dale is slumped in the chair behind the desk. You can't wake him.

On the ceiling, there's a carbon monoxide alarm. Its light is off. It runs on the hotel's power. No battery backup.

Never run a generator indoors. Not in a house, a garage, a basement, or a lobby. Not even with the door open. Outside only, at least twenty feet from doors, windows and vents.

Now you have two choices.

Stay inside, where it's warm, and the air is poison.

Or go out into a blizzard at three in the morning, where it's nine degrees.

What would you do?

You go.

If you think it's carbon monoxide, get outside to fresh air immediately. Then call nine-one-one.

=== CHUNK D ===
You shut the generator off. The silence is enormous.

Rosa leans on you like you're the railing. The others wake up dizzy and confused, and you herd them out under the porch roof. The snow is blowing sideways.

Dale is too heavy to carry. You grab him under the arms and drag him across the lobby floor. Inch by inch. Through the open door. Into the cold.

Lando walks ahead of you like he's leading a parade.

Your phone says nine percent. One bar. You call nine-one-one. It rings. And rings. Then a voice. The road is closed. They'll get to you as fast as they can.

And now there's a new problem. Nine degrees. Seven people in pajamas. One cat.

Shivering. Exhaustion. Confusion. Fumbling hands. Slurred speech. Drowsiness.

Half of those look exactly like carbon monoxide. You can't tell which one is hurting Rosa.

Your car is a white hump in the lot. You dig with your hands until you can't feel them. The door opens. The engine catches. Heat pours out. Four people pile in.

And Lando starts yowling again. The same yowl. He claws at the back window.

You look behind the car. The snow has drifted right over the tailpipe.

Stuck with your car in snow? Clear the snow off the exhaust pipe. Run the engine about ten minutes an hour, and crack a window.

You dig it out.

Lando was right. Again.

Five fifty a.m. Headlights on the mountain road. A plow. Behind it, an ambulance.

They give Dale oxygen right there in the snow. He opens his eyes. He looks at you. Then at the cat.

"No pets," he whispers.

Then. "He stays. Free. Forever."

Everyone made it.

Lando slept through the whole ambulance ride. On Rosa.

So be honest. Three in the morning. Nine degrees outside. Poison inside.

Would you have dragged everyone out into that blizzard? Or stayed where it was warm?

Tell me in the comments.

Next Monday, the lights go out somewhere new.
