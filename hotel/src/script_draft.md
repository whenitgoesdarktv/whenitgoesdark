# Snowed-In Hotel: working script draft (pre-research)

COLD OPEN
Two forty-seven in the morning. The power has been out for six hours.
Your cat's carrier is open. And empty.
Somewhere inside the wall, something is scratching.
It moves. Down the hallway. Past the ice machine. Past room fourteen.
Then it stops. Right behind your head.
The front desk told you nobody else is staying on this floor.
And downstairs, something has been running all night. You can feel it through the floor. A low, steady hum.
Before sunrise, that hum will almost kill everyone in this hotel.

TITLE: WHEN IT GOES DARK / A blizzard. A mountain hotel. No power.

CH1 THE LAST ROOM
Nine hours earlier. Six PM.
The Pinecrest Lodge sits halfway up a mountain road. The sign on the front door says NO PETS.
The bag on your shoulder says nothing. Because Lando is asleep inside it.
Lando is your cat. Lando has opinions about bags.
The storm got here first. The radio says the nor'easter is wrapping around the mountains, and the plows have stopped for the night. The road behind you is already gone.
The manager, Dale, slides a key across the desk. Room twenty-two. The last one.
He looks at your bag. The bag meows.
Dale says nothing. Dale has had a very long day.
Eight twelve PM. The lights flicker. Once. Twice.
Then the whole mountain goes dark.
No heat. No elevator. No Wi-Fi. Your phone says sixty-four percent.
And then, from downstairs, a motor coughs to life.
You look over the railing. Dale is dragging a generator in from the snow. Into the lobby. Right next to the fireplace.
He props the front door open with a boot.
"It's just for the coffee and the phones," he says. "Door's open. It's fine."
Remember that open door.

CH2 THE COLD
By ten, your room is fifty-eight degrees. By midnight, fifty-one.
[RULE: cold] Keep the heat where you are. Pick the smallest room. Shut the door, towels under it, every layer you own.
Lando ignores the rule. Lando sleeps on your face.
Twelve forty. A knock. It's the woman from room twelve. Rosa. Two scarves, a hotel bathrobe over her coat.
"Everybody's going down to the lobby," she says. "It's warm down there."
It is warm down there. Six guests asleep on the couches. The generator humming by the fire. The door open to the snow.
On the stairs, your head starts to ache. You figure it's the altitude. Or the day.
Rosa takes the couch closest to the generator.
You carry Lando back upstairs, because Lando will not stop yowling in that lobby. Every time you set him down, he pulls toward the door.
Cats hate loud machines. That's what you tell yourself.

CH3 SOMETHING IN THE WALLS
Two forty-seven AM. You wake up and the room is turning.
The carrier door is open. Lando is gone.
Then you hear it. Scratching. Inside the wall, right behind the bed.
You press your ear to the wallpaper. Claws. Moving. Fast. Toward the hallway.
You follow it with your headlamp. Twenty doors. Every one of them dark.
The scratching runs ahead of you, past the ice machine, all the way to the end of the hall. And stops under a metal grate in the floor.
A vent. Warm air is rising through it. From the lobby.
You kneel down. Two green eyes look back up at you.
Lando.
He isn't trying to get to you. He's trying to get away from that warm air.
You stand up too fast. The hallway tilts. Your stomach turns over. Your fingers fumble the grate.
For a second you think someone is standing at the far end of the hall. Your headlamp flickers. There's no one there.
The scratching was Lando.
The dizziness is not.

CH4 THE HUM
[RULE: CO] Carbon monoxide has no color and no smell. A generator makes a lot of it, fast, and it builds up inside even with a door open.
Headache. Dizziness. Weakness. An upset stomach. Confusion. It feels like the flu. And people who are asleep can die from it before they ever feel sick.
Six people are asleep in that lobby.
You go down the stairs with Lando in one arm and the railing in the other. Every step is heavier.
The lobby is warm. Too warm. The fire is down to coals. The generator is still humming.
"Rosa." She doesn't wake. You shake her shoulder. She opens her eyes and she doesn't know who you are.
Dale is slumped in the chair behind the desk. You can't wake him.
The little light on the ceiling is a smoke alarm. Not a carbon monoxide alarm. There isn't one.
[RULE: generator] A generator always goes outside. Never in a home, a garage, a basement, or a lobby. Not even with the doors and windows open. Far from every door and window.
Now you have two choices.
Stay inside, where it's warm and the air is poison.
Or go out into a blizzard at three in the morning, where it's nine degrees.
What would you do?
[CHOICE CARD: STAY INSIDE / GO OUT INTO THE STORM]

CH5 FRESH AIR
You go.
[RULE: CO response] If you think it's carbon monoxide, get to fresh air. Right now. Then call 911.
You shut the generator off. The silence is enormous.
Rosa leans on you like you're the railing. The others wake up dizzy and confused, and you herd them out under the porch roof. Snow is blowing sideways.
Dale is too heavy to carry. You grab him under the arms and drag him across the lobby floor, inch by inch, through the open door, into the cold.
Lando walks ahead of you like he's leading a parade.
Your phone says nine percent. One bar. You call 911. It rings. And rings. Then a voice. The roads are closed. They'll get to you as fast as they can.
Now there's a new problem. Nine degrees. Seven people in pajamas. One cat.
[RULE: hypothermia signs] Shivering. Exhaustion. Confusion. Fumbling hands. Slurred speech. Drowsiness. Half of those look exactly like carbon monoxide. You can't tell which one is hurting Rosa.
Your car is buried in the lot. You dig. You get the engine running. Heat pours out. Four people pile in.
And Lando starts yowling again. The same yowl. He claws at the back window.
You look behind the car. The snow has drifted right over the tailpipe.
[RULE: car in snow] In a snowed-in car, clear the exhaust pipe first. A buried tailpipe pushes carbon monoxide back into the car. Run the engine about ten minutes an hour, with a window cracked.
You dig it out with your bare hands.
Lando was right. Again.

FINALE MORNING
Five fifty AM. Headlights on the mountain road. A plow. Behind it, an ambulance.
They give Dale oxygen right there in the snow. He opens his eyes. He looks at you. Then at the cat.
"No pets," he whispers.
Then: "He stays. Free. Forever."
Everyone made it.
Lando slept through the whole ambulance ride. On Rosa.
So be honest. Three in the morning. Nine degrees outside. Poison inside.
Would you have dragged everyone out into that blizzard? Or stayed where it was warm?
Tell me in the comments.
[REAL LANDO END CARD]
